Boca questions

The three things people ask first.

Is my insurance going to drop me over the roof?

Florida law says: if your roof is under 15 years old, an insurance company can't drop you just for its age. Over 15, they have to let you get an inspection — and if it shows 5 or more years of life left, the roof can't be the reason.

Fla. Stat. 627.7011(5), for policies since July 1, 2022. Citizens — the state-run insurance company many Florida homeowners end up with — also asks for a 4-point inspection (a quick check of roof, electric, plumbing and A/C) on homes over 20 years old.

It's hurricane season. Will I get gouged?

Season runs June 1 to November 30, and out-of-town crews knock on doors after every big storm. Here you get a free look and a written price — and time to think it over. Same phone number before the storm and after.

Hurricane season dates: National Hurricane Center.

Do I really need a whole new roof?

Sometimes. A coating is right for some flat roofs and wrong for others. The free look tells you which. If it is a new roof, you hear that too — and you can get other bids.

A coating is off the table when the wood underneath is wet or rotten, the roof is past its life, or your insurance company says in writing that the roof has to be replaced.

    Coating, explained

    Silicone roof coating

    A silicone coating is like a thick, waterproof paint rolled over a flat roof that is still solid underneath. It seals the seams — the lines where two sheets of roofing overlap — and stops leaks without tearing anything off. No "tear-off," which is roofer talk for ripping the old roof down to the wood. Matt's coatings come with a 15-year warranty.

    • No tear-off. The old roof stays, so there's little to no demolition waste.
    • Seals the seams, the spot where flat roofs leak most.
    • Most coatings are light-colored, so they bounce South Florida sun instead of soaking it up.

    When a coating is not the answer

    • The wood under the roof is wet or rotten.
    • The roof is already past its life.
    • It's a steep shingle or tile roof.
    • Your insurance company says, in writing, that the roof has to be replaced.

    Matt needs to see the roof — and the seams — before he picks the material. We look first, then we price. If the roof needs replacing, he says so, and you can get other bids.

    What's in a roof

    Watch a roof go on.

    1. 01

      The wood

      Every roof sits on plywood. Soft or rotten boards have to be replaced before anything goes on top.

    2. 02

      The waterproof sheet

      A waterproof sheet goes over the wood — think of it as the roof's raincoat. An extra leak barrier goes along the edges and in the valleys, the low lines where two slopes of the roof meet and water runs together.

    3. 03

      The part you see

      Then the top layer: shingle, tile or metal. On a flat roof, a coating — a thick waterproof layer — goes right over the old surface instead.

    4. 04

      Sealed and checked

      The peak, the edges, and every pipe and chimney get sealed. Then the whole thing gets checked, top to bottom.

    Roofing, explained

    Eight words roofers say. What they mean.

    You don't need to know any of this to call. But if a roofer says one of these, now you'll know.

    01

    Coating

    A thick sealant rolled over a flat roof you already have, to stop leaks. Nothing gets torn off.

    02

    Flashing

    Metal strips where the roof meets walls, chimneys and pipes. Most leaks start here.

    03

    Pitch

    How steep the roof is. A flat roof can often be coated instead of replaced. A steep roof gets shingle, tile or metal.

    04

    Decking

    The plywood your roof sits on. If it is soft or rotten, a coating is off the table.

    05

    Tear-off

    Ripping the old roof down to the wood before a new one goes on.

    06

    Underlayment

    The paper-like sheet under shingles or tile. The roof's raincoat.

    07

    Wind mitigation

    An inspection that lists what your house has going for it in a hurricane: the shape of the roof, how it is strapped to the walls, shutters. Can lower your insurance bill.

    08

    4-point

    A quick inspection of four things: roof, electric, plumbing and A/C. Insurance companies want it on older homes.

    Roof types

    Which roof do you have?

    Look at it from the street. Here's how to tell — and what usually goes wrong with each.

    What it is

    A roof that is flat or nearly flat. Common on condos, additions, and commercial buildings.

    How to spot it

    From the street you can't see the roof at all — just a straight edge along the top of the wall.

    What usually goes wrong

    Water sits on it, and the seams (where two sheets of roofing overlap) open up. A coating seals those seams without tearing anything off.

    What it is

    Overlapping flat pieces, usually dark gray, brown or black. The most common roof in America.

    How to spot it

    A sloped roof with a fine, layered look, like fish scales.

    What usually goes wrong

    Shingles curl, crack or blow off in wind. Insurance companies watch the age of a shingle roof closely.

    What it is

    Curved or flat clay or concrete tiles. The classic South Florida look.

    How to spot it

    Orange, terracotta or gray, with a wavy or ridged surface you can see from the street.

    What usually goes wrong

    Tiles crack when someone walks on them, and the waterproof layer underneath wears out long before the tiles do.

    What it is

    Long metal panels with raised ribs. Shiny or painted.

    How to spot it

    Straight vertical lines running from the peak to the edge.

    What usually goes wrong

    Screws back out and the rubber washers dry up. Usually a repair, not a new roof.
